Tutorial

How to use the Attention Explorer

MultiFlow couples an RNA flow and an ATAC flow through the dual cross-attention of its cross-flow modules (CFMs). This page shows the attention of the second module (CFM2) as a 512 × 512 map of hidden elements and turns strong pairs into candidate peak–gene links: the ATAC element points to its top-attributed peaks, the RNA element to its top-attributed genes, and a peak near one of those genes gives a link. For a guided run through one example, close this panel and click “Play the example”.

Quick start

  1. Pick a datasetClick one of the three cards, then choose a cell type or context in the menu.
  2. Pick a pairClick a square of the map, or a gold chip under “From the manuscript”. The map zooms in on the pair.
  3. Read the keywordsThe panel on the right shows the pair as keywords. Click a gene, a GO term or a peak to open its track or table.
  4. Open the networkClick “Regulatory network”; hover a node to trace its paths, click it to select it.

  1. Datasets

    The three cards follow the manuscript figures.

    • Cell-state generation (OpenProblems multiome, Fig. 4b–e): the cell-state generation model on real cells, one map per cell type, 22 in all.
    • Perturbation state (Perturb-multiome, Fig. 4f–g): cells generated by the perturbation-prediction model after a TF knockout, 10 contexts.
    • Control vs perturbation (Perturb-multiome, Fig. 5): the model applied to non-targeting (NT) control and knockout cells, giving a control and a perturbation network, 10 contexts.

    Contexts tagged held-out cell type were left out of training (leave-one-cell-type-out); their control and knockout cells are generated. These contexts extend the manuscript figures, which use training contexts. The page address names the current map, so you can copy it to share that map.

  2. Attention map

    Rows are the 512 ATAC hidden elements (A0–A511) and columns the 512 RNA hidden elements (R0–R511). Each square is the dual cross-attention of one pair in CFM2 at flow time t = 0.90, averaged over cells. The mean of a map is exactly 2/512, so colour starts at this uniform level and saturates at the 99.9th percentile.

    • Hover a square for its attention, its fold over uniform and its rank among the 262,144 pairs; in control vs perturbation, for both values with their route ranks.
    • Click a square to select that pair; the map zooms in on it. Click a bar above or to the right to select that element with its strongest partner.
    • Zoom: when zoomed in, drag to move; use +, − and Whole map, or pinch (ctrl + wheel). Enlarge gives the map the full width.
    • Gold rings (gold squares when zoomed in) mark the manuscript examples. The checkbox next to the search box marks the top 0.1% pairs (cell-state generation), the top 100 routes (perturbation state) or the perturbation-only routes (control vs perturbation).
    • Show (control vs perturbation): Perturbation − control is the default; red pairs are stronger after the knockout, blue pairs stronger in the control, on a scale symmetric at the 99.9th percentile of the difference. Perturbation and Control show each map alone. The links, lists and track follow the control network in the Control view and the perturbation network otherwise.

  3. Peak–gene links

    Gradient-based attribution (Methods) gives each ATAC element a ranked list of peaks and each RNA element a ranked list of genes. A selected pair proposes a link wherever one of its peaks lies within 120 kb of a TSS of one of its genes.

    • TSS: the peak spans the gene’s TSS. Promoter: it overlaps the 3 kb upstream. Otherwise the distance is shown. The nearest TSS of the gene’s GENCODE v47 basic transcripts is used.
    • Links are ordered by class, then by path score = attention × peak attribution × gene attribution (attributions scaled to their list maximum).
    • The keyword board shows one chip per linked gene; the full list is in the Gene track view.
    • Gold rows are manuscript examples. A green dot means the peak overlaps the reference track (H3K27ac, or ChIP-seq of the perturbed factor); a coral dot marks a perturbation-only peak or gene. Hover a row for the peak and gene ranks.
    • Strongest peak–gene links (blue chips above the map): the best link of each of the strongest pairs, with distal links down-weighted, one chip per gene.

  4. Gene track

    Clicking a gene or peak keyword, a link, a peak or a gene opens the Gene track view and draws its locus.

    • Model link: the gold arc from the peak to the linked TSS; dashed arcs are other links of the same peak in this pair.
    • Genes: GENCODE v47 genes with strand and TSS arrow; linked genes are blue.
    • Peaks: the ATAC element’s attributed peaks (green, height ∝ attribution, top 10 ranked; gold = selected) over all peaks of the model (grey).
    • Reference: H3K27ac, or ChIP-seq of the perturbed factor. Zoom with − / + / Reset.

  5. RNA and ATAC elements

    • Partner chips: the element’s 5 strongest partners on the map; click one to switch the pair (grey = no attribution stored).
    • RNA element: its top 10 genes and its top 4 GO terms (MSigDB C5 GO:BP v2026.1, hypergeometric test against the model’s genes, terms of 15–500 genes). Hover a term for its counts; click it to list the genes.
    • GO terms view: all stored terms of the RNA element (up to 8) with the genes hit and the term size, the fold enrichment, P and q; click a row to list its genes.
    • ATAC element: its top 10 peaks, each named by the nearest TSS within 120 kb.
    • The element cards are in the Elements view. Clicking a gene or a peak there opens the gene track.

    Lists: Top 200 peaks and Top 100 genes (cell-state generation); the Fig. 4 gene sets of up to 200 genes for training contexts, otherwise the Top 100 genes (perturbation state); the Top 100 of each network (control vs perturbation). Attribution is stored only for elements of the top pairs or routes.

  6. Regulatory network

    Four layers built from the attention: peaks → ATAC elements → RNA elements → genes. Line width follows the attribution or attention weight within each layer.

  7. Search

    • A gene, such as HBG1: the RNA elements that rank it, best rank first.
    • A position or range, such as chr2:112836364: the ATAC elements with an attributed peak within 500 bp.
    • An element, such as A284 or R316: selects it with its strongest partner.

    Use ↑ ↓ and Enter to choose a hit; Esc closes the list. In control vs perturbation, each hit says whether it comes from the perturbation or the control network.

Terms

ATAC / RNA element
One of the 512 hidden elements of the ATAC or RNA flow in the CFM; A284 is ATAC element 284.
Attribution
Gradient-based scores (Methods) that tie each ATAC element to peaks and each RNA element to genes; the top-attributed lists feed the links.
Route
In the Perturb-multiome networks, an ATAC × RNA pair among the 1,000 strongest pairs of a network.
Path score
Attention × peak attribution × gene attribution.
Perturbation-only route
A route of the perturbation network that is not a route of the control network.
Perturbation-only gene / peak
A gene or peak attributed in the perturbation network but not in the control network.
Held-out cell type
A cell type left out of training; its cells are generated.
NT
Non-targeting control.
